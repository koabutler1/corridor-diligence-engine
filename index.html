Methodology — every number, its arithmetic

This page is the tool's contract: each metric's exact formula, aggregation rule, and refusal condition. Nothing on the Screen is computed any other way. Definitions marked definitional are implemented as stated but pending team confirmation before the screen is treated as a standard.
Data handling before any metric
Grain. One row per geography per quarter from a market-data export (.csv or .xlsx, read natively).
Forecast cap. Base Case exports include forecast quarters. If an As Of column is present, only actuals are screened: periods ≤ (As Of − 1 quarter). The cap is shown in the corridor builder.
Corridor. A corridor is a named selection of markets — checkboxes, a catalog default, or (deployed app) an AI proposal validated against the file's own market list and confirmed by the analyst. Membership is definitional.
Number parsing. "$", ",", "%", and whitespace are stripped; rates greater than 1 are treated as percentages (7.9 → 0.079) when converting to SF.
Refusal rule. Any metric whose inputs are missing renders "—" with a reason — the tool refuses rather than guesses.

Claims Reconciler — where AI does and does not operate
The model performs extraction only: it reads broker prose and returns structured claims (metric, value, quote). The verdict is arithmetic: |claim − engine value| compared to fixed thresholds — vacancy 1.0pp, rent growth 2.0pp, months of supply 2.0, institutional ownership 5.0pp (definitional). A claim about a metric the engine does not compute returns NO_ENGINE_VALUE rather than a judgment. The narrative generator drafts prose from computed figures only and is labeled for analyst review; the model never computes or adjusts a metric anywhere in this tool.
Excess-vacancy model — months of supply
A standard equilibrium-vacancy approach, computed per market: equilibrium vacancy = the market’s average vacancy rate over the baseline window; excess vacant SF = max(0, current rate − equilibrium) × inventory; excess-vacancy months of supply = (excess SF + under-construction SF) ÷ the window’s monthly absorption pace. The split matters: pipeline months burn off on a delivery schedule, standing-vacancy months clear only on demand’s. Definitional, like the simple metric it complements.
National benchmarking & verdict flags
When the loaded file contains many markets, the tool computes months of supply for every market (same formula, same window, same as-of) and reports the corridor's percentile — the share of markets with a lower value; higher percentile = looser. Verdict flags then become distribution-based: TIGHT = bottom quartile, LOOSE = top quartile of the file's markets, replacing the absolute 9/15 cutoffs (which remain the fallback for small files). The time-replay chart applies the fixed baseline to each quarter's vacant stock — a replay against the era's normal demand, not a per-quarter re-estimate. Definitional, like the rest.
Verification
Formulas were checked during development with unit tests against hand-computed expectations and parity checks between the JavaScript and Python implementations.
